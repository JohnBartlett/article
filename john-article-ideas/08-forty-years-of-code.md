# Forty Years of Code: A Chicago Engineer Looks Back

**Category:** Personal Essay
**Angle:** A first-person look at how technology work, and Chicago's tech scene, changed over one career.
**Timeliness:** Evergreen.

Note: in this piece you are the subject. Section 8 ("the subject gets the last word") becomes your own closing reflection, and quotes in section 4 can come from mentors or colleagues.

## 1. Hero photo
- An early computer, terminal, or punch card from your career alongside your current laptop, or a photo of you from early in your career.
- Caption: credit yourself or the original photographer.

## 2. Lede
- Hook: your first day as a programmer and what the machine in front of you could do.
- Narrow to the smaller story: one project or moment that shows how much has changed.

## 3. Scene
- Your first workplace: the room, the equipment, the people.

## 4. The central moment
- Something a mentor or colleague said that stayed with you. Quote it as exactly as you remember it, and say that it's from memory.

## 5. Why it matters
- What stayed the same across forty years. The craft, care, and getting things right, not the tools.

## 6. Secondary beats
- The Chicago tech landmarks you saw firsthand.
- A funny failure from early in your career.
- Building this magazine as the most recent chapter.

## 7. The Chicago turn
- Chicago's tech identity has always been practical and industrial, less flashy than the coasts.

## 8. Close
- Your own reflection on what you'd tell someone starting out now.

## Photos needed
- Personal archive photos and period equipment.

## Interviews / sources
- You, and optionally a former colleague or mentor.
