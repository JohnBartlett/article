# Keeping a Magazine Honest in the Age of Machines

**Category:** From the Editors
**Angle:** Why the magazine publishes contributors' words verbatim, credits every photo, and checks every caption, and how the tools help and sometimes get in the way.
**Timeliness:** Evergreen. It could run with #1, "Behind the Sunday Edition."

## 1. Hero photo
- A contributor's original email beside the published page (with the contributor's permission), or a marked-up proof.
- Caption: your own photo or screenshot.

## 2. Lede
- Hook: every week, a small team turns dozens of emails into a finished magazine, and one wrong caption can hurt a contributor's trust.
- Narrow to the smaller story: one near-miss that was caught (anonymized, or used with permission).

## 3. Scene
- Saturday production: the source emails open on one side and the built page on the other.

## 4. The central moment
- A contributor on why seeing their words published exactly as written matters to them. Quote them verbatim, with permission.

## 5. Why it matters
- Readers trust names, dates, and credits. Accuracy is a form of respect for writers and subjects.

## 6. Secondary beats
- Why photo filenames are never renamed.
- How a dropped italic or a lost link can change a sentence's meaning.
- The honest limits of automated checks.

## 7. The Chicago turn
- Chicago's newspaper tradition, from the City News Bureau's "If your mother says she loves you, check it out."

## 8. Close
- A contributor or Judy gets the last word.
