# "Space Command": Zenith and the Invention of the TV Remote

**Category:** Chicago History / Technology
**Angle:** A West Side company invented the TV remote, and the engineer behind it spent decades as an unsung Chicagoan.
**Timeliness:** Evergreen. It could be paired with fall TV season or the holidays ("the gift that changed the living room").

## 1. Hero photo
- A Zenith Space Command remote, or a 1950s Zenith print ad.
- Caption: collector or archive credit.

## 2. Lede
- Hook: whoever holds the remote rules the house, and the remote was invented in Chicago.
- Narrow to the smaller story: Robert Adler and the chime-bar remote that needed no batteries.

## 3. Scene
- Zenith's headquarters on the West Side in the 1950s, and Commander Eugene McDonald's campaign against TV commercials.

## 4. The central moment
- The engineering problem and Adler's solution, with quotes from interviews Adler gave later in life, cited verbatim.

## 5. Why it matters
- The "clicker" name came from this device, and every remote since traces back to it.

## 6. Secondary beats
- Eugene Polley's earlier Flash-Matic, which used light and was confused by sunlight.
- Dogs reacting to the ultrasonic tones.
- Zenith's later decline and sale.

## 7. The Chicago turn
- Chicago was once a consumer-electronics capital: Zenith, Motorola, and Admiral.
