# Behind the Sunday Edition

**Category:** From the Editors
**Angle:** How a contributor's email becomes the magazine readers open on Sunday morning, told as a newsroom story rather than a technical one.
**Timeliness:** Evergreen. It would work well near an anniversary or a milestone edition count.

## 1. Hero photo
- The homepage on a laptop at the kitchen table on a Sunday morning, or a Saturday-night desk with the edition half built.
- Caption: credit the photographer, even if it's you.

## 2. Lede
- Hook: every Sunday a complete magazine appears, and most readers never think about how it got there.
- Narrow to the smaller story: follow one article from one writer through a single week.

## 3. Scene
- Judy's lineup email arrives early in the week.
- Contributors send text and photos from all over: Paris, Antibes, the North Shore, a UIC classroom.
- Saturday is the real production day.

## 4. The central moment
- Pick one real article from a past edition and trace it from arrival to Sunday.
- Quote the writer (with permission) about what it felt like to see it published.
- Quote Judy on how she picks the lineup.

## 5. Why it matters
- A small, volunteer-driven magazine lives or dies on care: getting names, captions, and photo credits exactly right.

## 6. Secondary beats
- The photo that arrived sideways and had to be fixed.
- A caption email that came after the article did.
- Writers who send pieces from their phones.

## 7. The Chicago turn
- Chicago has a long tradition of civic-minded small publications. Classic Chicago continues it with modern tools.

## 8. Close
- Give Judy or Megan the last word on why they started the magazine.

## Photos needed
- The production setup, a screenshot of an edition going live, and a portrait of Judy and Megan.

## Interviews / sources
- Judy Carmack Bross, Megan McKinney, and one long-running contributor (Bob Glaze or Susan Aurinko).
