Dr. Rebecca Tilles: A European Soul, Born in ChicagoBy Dr. Robert Leo Murphy

Rating: Strong

Murphy has assembled a genuinely illuminating portrait of a Chicagoan who has built one of the more quietly remarkable careers in the transatlantic decorative arts world. The Q&A format serves the material well: Tilles is articulate and specific, and her trajectory—from the Latin School to Wellesley to Bard to the MFA Boston to Hillwood to L’ÉCOLE in Paris—reads as both logical and quietly astonishing. The piece succeeds because Tilles herself is a compelling subject and Murphy gives her room to speak at length.

The strongest exchanges are those where Tilles reflects on Chicago’s place in her formation—her description of the Art Institute as a "second home" and her affection for the city’s undervalued decorative arts collections give the piece its emotional core. The framing of Chicago-born ambition as distinctly European in sensibility is an idea worth developing further.

Editorial Note: The piece occasionally loses forward momentum in the middle section, where the Q&A lingers on institutional logistics (exhibition timelines, Van Cleef & Arpels partnerships) at the expense of the more personal and atmospheric passages. Readers drawn in by the portrait of a Chicago girl in Paris will want more of those vivid details—the Galerie Aveline vernissage, the Versailles gala—and less curriculum vitae recitation. Murphy might also consider a brief orienting paragraph before the interview proper: first-time readers of Classic Chicago may need a sentence or two to understand what L’ÉCOLE is and why it matters.
