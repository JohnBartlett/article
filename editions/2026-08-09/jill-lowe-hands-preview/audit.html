Editorial Audit

“A Helping Hand” vs. Original PDF

A line-by-line comparison of the laid-out article against the source PDF, prepared for review.

Bottom line: the article is faithful in structure and content — nothing was dropped, no paragraphs were cut, and all 25 supplied photos are placed. However, a number of small text changes were made silently during layout that should have been flagged instead of corrected outright. They're itemized below for your review and sign-off.

Duplicated text removed

These go beyond typo-fixing — actual repeated words or list entries were trimmed.

  • Dupe “not everyone knows that know that BSL and ASL are different” → the duplicated “know that” was removed.
  • Dupe “I insert here a story here concerning…” → one of the two “here”s was removed.
  • Dupe “Heavy handed” appeared twice in the closing expressions list in the original — the article includes it only once.

Section headings reformatted

Structure Headings were shortened and re-cased for the web layout. For example, the PDF’s own section title —

“BSL (British Sign Language) and ASL (American Sign Langage)”

— became the shorter heading “BSL and ASL”, with the full names of both languages moved into the body paragraph instead. Section headings generally were also shifted from sentence case to Title Case to match the site’s house style.

Photo placement correction

Corrected: the PDF's Shibboleth section calls for two side-by-side photos — “None-German way of indicating three” and “German way of indicating three.” Both stock photos were in fact supplied in the original batch, but one (the German-style count — thumb, index, and middle finger) was initially misplaced in the Hand Signals section as generic filler instead of recognized for what it was. It has been moved to sit alongside the non-German photo in the Shibboleth section, as originally intended. (A stand-in photo John took himself to cover the presumed gap was removed once the correct original photo was found.)

What was not changed

  • No paragraphs, sentences, or sections were cut.
  • No content was added beyond the one requested intro line before “Understood Expressions.”
  • All 25 supplied photos (21 stock + 4 personal) are placed somewhere in the article.
  • Photo captions match the PDF exactly where the PDF specified one; photos without a given caption were left uncaptioned rather than invented.
  • The personal anecdote about your father and niece Tess uses your exact caption text (typo corrected as noted above).
