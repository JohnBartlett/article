Key Findings
1
Biba Roesch has a loyal, clicking audience

Biba’s Favorite Things: Palm Springs (169 views) and Linda Heister (52 views in the launch week) both performed above the edition average. Her readers don’t just open the email — they click through to the article and read it. She also drove the most Heritage Auctions ad impressions (167) of any article in March 29.

2
DateBook is a returning resource, not a one-time read

The March 8 DateBook pulled 247 views — second only to Rebecca Tilles in that edition. Readers bookmark and return to it throughout the week. Every edition should have one.

3
People profiles are the strongest content category

Four of the top five all-time articles are profiles: Alicia Ziegler (410), Rebecca Tilles (288), Cynthia Safford Mulcahy (280), and Children’s Research Fund (250). Named subjects bring their own networks.

4
Alicia Ziegler is the all-time outlier — worth understanding why

Alicia Ziegler: The Artful Concierge received 410 views — 43% more than the next best article. Something drove outsized traffic: a social share, a personal email forward, or Alicia’s own network. Understanding what triggered it could inform how we promote future profiles.

5
March editions are running ~25% below the February peak

Feb 15–Mar 8 averaged roughly 1,100 article views per edition. March 15 onward has averaged around 800. This could reflect the audience still growing during February, seasonal patterns, or the mix of content types. It’s worth watching over the next two editions to see if April recovers.

6
Heritage Auctions ad: 638 impressions, all from March 29

The HA ad was only active during the March 29 edition in this 30-day window. Biba Palm Springs delivered the most impressions (167), followed by Unsung Gems Backgammon (139) and Driehaus Museum (107). The ad is performing well within its placement — readers who reach those articles are seeing it.
