On the northeast corner of Astor and Goethe, a block from the lake, stands one of the Gold Coast’s most private addresses: an eleven-residence Art Deco cooperative that Philip B. Maher designed in 1928. Inside it is a home that has had only three owners in nearly a century. Its next owner will be the fourth.

The residence was created for the Potter Palmer family by David Adler, the architect of many of the North Shore’s great estates. It spreads across two principal levels and roughly 7,000 square feet, and it is now coming to market through @properties.

“A home that has had only three owners in nearly a century. Its next owner will be the fourth.”

The Building

Maher, the great designer of Chicago’s Art Deco co-ops, finished 1301 North Astor in 1929: fifteen stories and just eleven residences, on one of the city’s most storied streets. He would return to the same intersection to design a second cooperative diagonally across the corner, and the pair still frame Astor and Goethe today.

Original buyers were invited to shape their own apartments, and the Potter Palmers II turned to Adler. The result is less an apartment than a townhouse lifted into the sky, beginning the moment the elevator opens: Adler designed the mirrored and steel elevator entry himself.

The Main Level

The foyer rises nineteen feet, and a curved staircase, now historically designated, sweeps up from it. Beyond lies a living room large enough for two fireplaces, framed by Aegean marble columns brought from the Palmer estate, with room for formal dining. A paneled library, with a fireplace of its own and a powder room, makes a natural study. The kitchen, a laundry room and two en-suite bedrooms complete the floor.

Upstairs

The upper level is given over to private rooms: a primary suite with its own fireplace, walk-in closet, dressing rooms and bath, and three further bedrooms, two with their own baths and one that would serve equally well as a study. More staircases link the levels, in the manner of a house rather than a flat.

A Museum of Details

What sets the home apart is how much of the past it has kept. There are original African ebony floors and French château wood floors, carved woodwork, ornate fireplaces, Art Deco bathrooms and lighting, Delft tiles, and marble and granite throughout. Most remarkable of all, a door, a fireplace, paneling and millwork from Ulysses S. Grant’s suite at the Palmer House Hotel were built into the rooms. Chicago magazine once named it among the five finest houses in the city.

The Neighborhood

Astor Street is the Gold Coast at its most elegant, a quiet run of mansions and prewar co-ops between Division Street and North Avenue. The lakefront is a block away, and Oak Street Beach and the Magnificent Mile are a short walk south.
