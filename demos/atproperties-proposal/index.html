The Idea

Great Houses, Told as Stories

Classic Chicago is a weekly digital magazine about the city’s culture, society, dining and architecture, published every Sunday. Our readers are the people who live in, buy and care about houses like the David Adler residence at 1301 North Astor.

We propose a new section, Chicago Architecture & Historic Homes, Presented by @properties, that gathers our architecture coverage in one place, and within it a Featured Residence: a full magazine feature on a property @properties is selling, written by our editors and clearly labeled as presented by @properties. The first would be 1301 North Astor, a home with only three owners in nearly a century, designed for the Potter Palmers II and later owned by Suzette Morton Davidson.

This page shows both pieces as working pages, explains each part of the layout, and sets out the ways we could promote the property.

Part One

The Section Page

A permanent home for Classic Chicago’s writing on landmarks, neighborhoods and great houses. It grows every week as new articles run, and @properties is its presenting sponsor.

Live demo: Architecture & Historic HomesOpen full page →
  1. Section header. “Chicago Architecture & Historic Homes” with “Presented by” and the @properties logo, so the sponsorship is the first thing a reader sees.
  2. Featured Residence banner. A wide photo of the current featured property with its headline and a “View the residence” button that opens the full feature. It changes whenever @properties features a new home.
  3. From Our Pages. Our own architecture articles, from Bob Glaze’s Gold Coast walk to Glessner House and Hawthorne Works. This is the editorial heart of the page and the reason readers come back.
  4. Featured Residence block. A compact summary in the magazine’s style: two photos, a few lines of history, the key facts (bedrooms, baths, size, price, agent) and a button to the feature.
  5. End-of-article strip. A one-line banner that closes every architecture article, “Presented by @properties · Featured Residence: 1301 North Astor Street,” so readers who arrive from the homepage or social media also see the property.
Part Two

The Featured Residence

A full feature of about 2,500 words on the property, written by the editors of Classic Chicago in the magazine’s voice, with @properties’ branding framing it. It reads as a story about a landmark home, not a classified listing, which is why readers finish it.

Live demo: A Residence at 1301 North AstorOpen full page →
  1. Sponsor bar. Black, with “Featured Residence · Presented by” and the @properties logo in white and red, taken from @properties’ own palette.
  2. Full-width hero. The building at Astor and Goethe, with the title “A Residence at 1301 North Astor” and a one-line summary.
  3. Fact strip. Eight bedrooms, six and a half baths, 7,000 square feet, 19-foot ceilings, $2.5 million, at a glance.
  4. The story. By the Editors of Classic Chicago Magazine: a tour of the apartment room by room, then its history, with the Palmers and the Gold Coast, Ulysses S. Grant’s Palmer House suite, Philip Maher, David Adler, the Mortons, Astor Street and Goudy Square across the street.
  5. Photo gallery. Exterior photographs of the building and its neighbor. The listing’s twenty interior photographs, supplied by @properties, would run here and through the article.
  6. A chronology. Twenty-one dated moments from 1847 to the listing on October 26, 2026, which turns the home’s provenance into something a reader can scan.
  7. More from Classic Chicago. Links to our related articles, which keep readers on the page and in the magazine.
  8. Schedule a Private Showing. A black @properties panel with the address, price, key facts, Margaret Baczkowski’s name and number, and buttons to the listing and to call.
  9. Disclosure. A line stating that the feature is presented by @properties, that Classic Chicago wrote it, and that listing details come from @properties.
Promotion

How We Could Advertise the Property

The feature is the centerpiece. Around it, the magazine can put the residence in front of readers in several ways, alone or as a package.

Feature

The Featured Residence

The full article above, written by our editors, published in a Sunday edition and kept permanently in the section.

Sponsorship

Section Sponsor

“Presented by @properties” on the Architecture & Historic Homes page and at the end of every architecture article, by the month.

Homepage

A Sunday Homepage Card

The feature runs as a card in that week’s edition, with the building’s photo and a “Presented by @properties” label.

Design

We Design the Ads

Banners, the Featured Residence block and the showing panel are designed by Classic Chicago to match the magazine, so @properties need not supply artwork beyond photos and a logo.

Events

DateBook and a Talk

Open houses and broker events can be listed in our weekly DateBook, and Judy Carmack Bross, a Chicago social historian, can speak about the building’s history, as she has been invited to do for 1301 North Astor.

Social

Our Social Channels

Posts on Classic Chicago’s Facebook, Instagram, X and Pinterest pointing to the feature, with photos of the house.

Subscribers

Subscriber Email

A mention in our notes to subscribers, who sign up on the magazine’s site, as that list grows.

Reporting

Monthly Results

Every button and banner carries tracking, and we report readers and clicks through to the listing each month, so @properties can see what the placement delivers.

Series

More Residences

Each new listing @properties wants to feature becomes the next Featured Residence, and the section keeps the earlier ones as an archive of great Chicago houses.

Standards

How We Keep It Classic Chicago

  • Sponsored pages are always labeled “Presented by @properties,” and the feature carries a disclosure line.
  • Our editors write the article and check the history against published sources; listing details come from @properties and are credited to them.
  • The rest of the section remains our own editorial work. If we write about a property @properties is selling outside the Featured Residence, it is labeled the same way.

What We Would Need from @properties

  • The listing’s interior photographs and the floor plan, with permission to publish them.
  • Logo files and any brand guidelines.
  • A quote from the listing agent or the owner, and confirmation of the facts, including the dates of each owner.
  • The listing link to use, and the open house or broker event dates, if any.

Next Steps

  1. Agree on the package and the term of the sponsorship.
  2. @properties sends photos, logo files and the agent’s quote.
  3. We finalize the feature and the section, and @properties reviews the facts.
  4. The feature runs in a Sunday edition, and the section goes live.
